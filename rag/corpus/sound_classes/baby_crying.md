# Baby Crying

## Description
Infant crying is a variable, high-pitched vocalisation used to signal distress, hunger, discomfort, or a need for attention. Durations range from a few seconds to many minutes.

## Typical Scenarios
- Hunger (typically rhythmic, repetitive cry)
- Discomfort from a wet nappy, cold, or gas pain
- Overstimulation or tiredness
- Illness or pain (often higher pitch, more urgent)
- Normal waking from sleep

## Safety Implications
**HIGH.** Prolonged crying (> 10 minutes without interruption) may indicate the infant needs urgent attention.
- Crying that is unusually high-pitched, continuous, or accompanied by other symptoms may indicate illness — consult a healthcare provider if concerned.
- For deaf and hard-of-hearing caregivers, SoundSight's immediate alert on detection is a key safety feature.
- A sudden, brief cry followed by silence is normal and may not require immediate response.

## Common False Positives
- A cat or other pet vocalising at a similar pitch
- TV or music audio containing baby sounds
- A crying child (older) with a similar frequency profile
- Certain wind or environmental sounds in the right frequency range
