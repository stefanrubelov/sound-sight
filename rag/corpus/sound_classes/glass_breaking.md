# Glass Breaking

## Description
The sound of glass shattering — a sharp, high-frequency crash followed by tinkling fragments. Duration is typically very short (< 2 seconds).

## Typical Scenarios
- A glass or bottle dropped and broken in the kitchen
- A window broken by wind, impact, or an object thrown
- A forced entry attempt (break-in)
- A mirror or glass picture frame falling from a wall

## Safety Implications
**CRITICAL.** The safety implication depends heavily on context.
- If combined with other anomalies (e.g., unusual time of night, no prior activity), treat as a potential break-in and contact emergency services.
- If detected during daytime domestic activity, the cause is likely accidental.
- Broken glass poses a physical injury risk — check for injuries before cleaning up.
- If a window is broken, secure the opening to prevent weather ingress or further intrusion.

## Common False Positives
- Metal utensils striking a hard surface producing a similar high-frequency ring
- A ceramic bowl or plate breaking (similar frequency profile to glass)
- TV or music audio containing glass-break sound effects
- Children's toys or games with chiming sounds
