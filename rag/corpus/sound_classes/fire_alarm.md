# Fire Alarm

## Description
A fire alarm is a safety device that detects smoke, heat, or carbon monoxide and emits a loud, sustained siren to alert occupants to potential fire danger.

## Typical Scenarios
- Cooking smoke triggering a kitchen detector
- Actual fire or smouldering material in the home
- Smoke from a neighbour's unit travelling through vents
- Low battery causing intermittent chirping (shorter, quieter pattern)
- Scheduled self-test by the alarm unit

## Safety Implications
**CRITICAL.** Always treat a fire alarm as a real emergency until confirmed otherwise.
- Evacuate immediately following your home's fire escape plan.
- Do not re-enter until the building is declared safe by emergency services.
- Call emergency services (112 / 911) if you cannot identify the cause quickly.
- If the event is brief (< 5 seconds) and does not repeat, it may be a test or low-battery chirp.

## Common False Positives
- Steam from a shower or boiling water near a detector
- Burnt toast, overcooked food, or a very smoky oven
- Aerosol sprays (hairspray, paint) near a smoke detector
- Dust accumulation in an aging sensor
- Sensor malfunction or end-of-life (detectors should be replaced every 10 years)
